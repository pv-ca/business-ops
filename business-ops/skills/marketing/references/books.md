# Books and frameworks: Marketing

This plugin carries insights drawn from these books, written in its own words; it does not include their text. Apply each framework from what you know of it, name the book behind a recommendation, and never copy passages.

| Book | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| Obviously Awesome (April Dunford) | positioning |
| Breakthrough Advertising (Eugene Schwartz) | levels of awareness and desire |
| Contagious (Jonah Berger) | why ideas spread |
| Hacking Growth (Ellis and Brown) | growth experimentation |
| The Referral Engine (John Jantsch) | referrals |

## Pocket summary

- **Positioning comes first (Dunford):** a channel amplifies a message. If the customer cannot tell what the product is and why it is different, more reach buys more confusion.
- **Match the awareness level (Schwartz):** speak about the problem to people who do not yet know solutions exist, and about the product only to people who already do.
- **Why ideas spread (Berger):** social currency, triggers, emotion, public visibility, practical value and stories.
- **Growth as experiments (Ellis and Brown):** pick one metric, list ideas, rank them by impact, confidence and ease, run a few per cycle and keep what the data supports.
- **Referral system (Jantsch):** referrals come from an experience worth mentioning plus a moment when the company asks. Build the ask into the customer journey.
