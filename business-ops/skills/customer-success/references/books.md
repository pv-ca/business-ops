# Methods and books: customer success

The working file is `customer-success-lifecycle-playbook.md` in this folder. This plugin carries insights drawn from the books, in its own words, and does not include their text. Rely on the playbook and consult an original book only when the user asks for fidelity to a method or the playbook does not resolve the case (section 44 of the playbook).

| Book | Use it for | Playbook |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Demand-Side Sales 101 (Bob Moesta) | The "big hire" (the purchase) and the "little hires" (each use after it); why the customer bought | Sections 4 and 5 |
| Gap Selling (Keenan) | Carrying the sales diagnosis into onboarding: current state, future state, expected outcome | Parts I and III |
| Blueprints for a SaaS Sales Organization (Jacco van der Kooij) | Segmentation, service models, capacity | Parts II and VIII |
| Deploy Empathy (Michele Hansen) | Listening to the customer without defending or selling; cancellation interviews | Parts VII and VIII |

## Pocket summary

- **Big hire and little hires:** the customer decides once to buy and then decides again, at each use, whether to continue. Retention is winning the little decisions.
- **Expectation debt:** whatever the sale promised beyond what delivery fulfils is collected later, with interest, as frustration and cancellation.
- **Activation:** the event that separates those likely to stay from those likely to leave. It must be observable and must predict continuation.
- **Value realized and value recognized:** it is not enough for the customer to gain; they need to see that they gained.
- **Churn has an old cause:** the reason stated at cancellation is rarely the cause. It usually lies in customer fit, expectation or activation.
