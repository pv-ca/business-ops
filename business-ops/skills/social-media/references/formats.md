# Formats

How to build each kind of piece. Structures and hooks are starting shapes: fill them with a specific fact, number, scene or story from the company's own context, or they read as templates.

## Contents

- One idea per piece

- Text post

## One idea per piece

Write the idea as one sentence before writing the piece: "Marcar a aula no mesmo horário toda semana ajuda mais do que estudar muito num dia só." If it takes two sentences, it is two pieces. Everything in the piece serves that sentence.

## Hooks

The opening decides whether the rest is seen. Write three to five options per piece and pick with the user. Useful angles:

| Angle | Shape | When it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Question the audience already asks | Their own words, as they would type them | Answering pre-purchase doubts |
| Specific result | A concrete outcome and how long it took | When there is a real, authorized case |
| Common mistake | What most people do and what it costs | Teaching content |
| Scene | A moment the reader recognizes from their own week | Building identification |
| Counter-intuitive claim | A belief of the field, challenged, with the reason | Point-of-view content, sparingly |
| Promise of the piece | What they will be able to do after reading | How-to and lists |

A hook that the piece does not pay off is bait: it wins the click and loses the follower.

## Text post

1. Hook: one or two lines that stand alone in the preview.
2. Context: the situation, in two or three sentences.
3. The point: the lesson, method or story, with one concrete example.
4. Close: the takeaway in one line and the call to action.

Break paragraphs where a person would pause. Short is fine; padded is not.

## Carousel

A carousel is a small argument, one step per slide. Decide the architecture before writing:

| Architecture | Slides | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steps | Cover, one step per slide, recap | How to do something |
| Problem to proof | Cover, the problem, why it happens, the way out, evidence, next step | Showing a method or a product |
| List | Cover, one item per slide, which one to start with | Tips, mistakes, tools |
| Before and after | Cover, the before, what changed, the after, how | Cases and transformations |
| Walkthrough | Cover, one screen or moment per slide | Product demos, a day in the service |

Rules for every architecture:

- The cover is the hook; it must work without the caption.
- One idea and few words per slide, large enough to read on a phone.
- Each slide gives a reason to swipe to the next.
- The last slide carries the single call to action.
- For each slide write the text and what the visual shows, using the brand identity file.

## Short video

Write for a viewer with the sound off who will decide in the first seconds.

| Beat | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Opening | The hook, said, shown and written on screen at the same time |
| Setup | Why this matters to the viewer, in one sentence |
| Delivery | The point, in steps or as one story, one visual change per beat |
| Close | The takeaway and the call to action |

Structures that fit the delivery: problem and solution, a list of three, a tutorial that shows the result first, a short story with a turn.

Deliver the script as a table: beat, what is said, what is on screen, text overlay. Add captions always. Say who appears and what needs to be recorded. Animated or screen-based videos go to `video-motion`.

## Caption

The caption supports the image or video; it does not repeat it.

1. First line: the hook, or the line that makes the image make sense.
2. Body: the context the visual could not carry.
3. Call to action.

## Call to action

One per piece, matched to how close the reader is:

| Reader | Ask for |
| --- | --- |
| Does not know the company | Follow, save, share |
| Knows it, not yet considering | Comment, answer a question, read more |
| Considering | Send a message, book a trial or a demo, see the plans |

## Patterns that sound generated

Readers recognize these and scroll past. Rewrite when a draft has them:

- A contrast staged as a reveal ("Não é X. É Y."). State Y, with the reason.
- A question the next line answers ("O resultado? ..."). Say the result.
- One short sentence per line for the whole post.
- Lists of three by reflex, and three-part closers.
- An emoji opening every line.
- Closers that ask for agreement ("Concorda?", "Faz sentido?").
- Invented vulnerability: a dramatic personal confession with no real story behind it.
- Inflated words: revolucionar, transformar, potencializar, destravar, jornada, "no mundo de hoje".
- Dashes used as dramatic pauses.

When the `humanizer` skill is available, run the draft through it.

## Check before delivering

- The piece has one idea, and the hook is paid off.
- It sounds like the company's voice file, not like the previous piece.
- Every fact, price and feature is confirmed in the context or catalog.
- No person is identifiable without authorization.
- The text fits the platform's current limits (checked, not remembered).
- There is one call to action.
