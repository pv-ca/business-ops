## Pillar matrix

Pillars are the three to five themes the company returns to. Formats are the shapes a theme can take. Crossing them turns "what do I post?" into a grid to fill.

Write the matrix in `social/estrategia.md`:

| Pillar | Serves the objective by | Share | Example ideas |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Teach | Showing competence before the sale | 35% | ... |
| Answer | Removing the doubts that stop a purchase | 25% | ... |
| Show the work | Building trust in how it is done | 20% | ... |
| Proof | Showing it works for people like them | 10% | ... |
| Offer | Asking for the next step | 10% | ... |

The names and shares above are an example. Derive the real ones from the objective: a profile meant to bring leads carries more answers and proof; one meant to keep customers carries more teaching and community. Keep the offer pillar small.

Then cross each pillar with the formats the team can produce (text post, carousel, short video, story, live) and write one idea per cell. Twenty cells is a month of content.

## Where ideas come from

In order of value, because the first ones are in the audience's own words:

1. Questions customers and leads actually asked: sales conversations, support messages, comments. Ask the user for them, or read the lead register and the document library when the index names them.
2. Objections heard before a purchase and reasons given for cancelling.
3. The catalog and the changelog: each available entry and each recent change is a piece.
4. The process library: how the company does something is "show the work" content.
5. Results and stories, with authorization.
6. The calendar of the audience: seasons, deadlines and dates that make them decide.
7. What is being discussed in the field this week. Search the web and cite the source; never invent a trend.

## The standing calendar

`social/calendario.md` is one table that grows. Newest period on top.

| Date | Channel | Format | Pillar | Idea (one sentence) | Source | Status | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-03-03 | Instagram | Carousel | Answer | ... | lead question, Feb | approved | |

Status values, in order: `idea`, `planned`, `drafted`, `approved`, `published`, `skipped`. Only the user moves a row to `approved` or `published`. A skipped row keeps its reason in the Result column; a pattern of skips says the cadence is too high.

On each run:

1. Read the table. Say what was published since last time, what is approved and waiting, and where the next gap is.
2. Ask about anything dated that the calendar missed (a launch, an event, a holiday the business observes).
3. Add rows until two to four weeks ahead are planned, keeping the pillar shares close to the strategy.
4. Fill the Result column for published rows when the user brings numbers.

## Cadence and batching

- Set the weekly number from the hours the team has, then hold it. Start low and raise it only after a month kept without skips.
- Batch: one session to plan the fortnight, one to write, one to record or design. Switching between planning and producing every day is what breaks routines.
- Keep two or three approved evergreen pieces in reserve for the week when nothing else gets done.

## Repurposing

Produce one substantial piece and derive the rest. A long explanation, a recorded class, a customer conversation or a process document can each yield:

| Derived piece | Take |
| --- | --- |
| Text post | The single strongest point |
| Carousel | The steps or the list |
| Short video | One moment, one tip or one answer, under a minute |
| Story | The behind-the-scenes of making it |
| Message to current customers | The practical part, without the pitch |

Mark derived rows in the Source column with the row they came from, so a result can be traced to the original idea.

## Monthly review

Bring the platform's own numbers; estimates are not data.

| Objective | Look at | Not at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reach new people | Views from non-followers, shares, new followers | Likes |
| Build trust | Saves, comments with substance, profile visits, watch time | Follower count |
| Generate leads | Messages received, link clicks, trials or demos booked, where each lead says they came from | Reach |
| Keep customers | Replies and questions from customers, attendance, renewals mentioned | Views |

Then:

1. Rank the month's pieces by the metric of the objective, and tag each with its pillar and format.
2. Name what the top three share and what the bottom three share.
3. Decide: repeat (what), stop (what), test (one experiment, with the criterion that will count as success).
4. Record the decision and its date at the top of `social/estrategia.md`. If the same pillar underperforms for two months, change the strategy rather than the captions.

A small account produces small samples. Say when a difference is too small to mean anything, and prefer patterns across several pieces to the result of one.
