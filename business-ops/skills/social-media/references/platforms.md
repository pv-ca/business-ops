## Choosing channels

Answer three questions from the company context before naming a platform:

1. Where does this audience already look for this kind of thing, or stumble on it?
2. Who decides the purchase, and is it the same person who sees the post?
3. What can the team produce every week without burning out: text, images, someone on camera, screen recordings?

Pick one main channel and at most one secondary. A secondary channel earns its place when most of its content is the main channel's content adapted, not a second production line.

## What each platform is for

| Platform | People go there to | Rewards | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Instagram | Follow people and brands they like, discover through short video | Short video for reach, carousels for saves and shares, Stories for closeness with existing followers | Services to consumers, visual products, local and personal brands |
| TikTok | Be entertained and learn in seconds, from strangers | The first seconds, watch time, a plain and direct style | Consumer reach, younger audiences, teaching content |
| YouTube Shorts | Same habit as TikTok, inside a search engine | Watch time, plus titles people search for | Evergreen how-to that should be found later |
| LinkedIn | Follow their field and their peers | Specific professional experience, document carousels, comments that turn into conversation | B2B, hiring, founders and specialists speaking as themselves |
| Facebook | Keep up with groups, family, local life | Groups and local community, native video | Local businesses, older audiences, communities |
| WhatsApp (status, channels, broadcast lists) | Talk to people they already know | Usefulness and restraint; people leave lists that spam | Keeping current customers and warm leads close |

A person's profile usually reaches further than a company page on the same platform, because people follow people. Decide with the user who speaks: the brand, the founder, a teacher, a specialist.

## B2C service sold on trust

Examples: lessons, consulting to individuals, health, beauty, any service where the buyer chooses a person.

- The buyer is deciding whether to trust this provider with their time and money. Content shows the provider at work: how a session goes, how a difficulty is handled, what progress looks like.
- Short video with a real person outperforms polished graphics, because the product is the person.
- Useful pillars: teaching a small thing the audience can use today, answering the questions asked before buying, showing the method, real stories of progress (with authorization), the offer.
- The call to action is a conversation: a message, a trial session. Make it one tap away.
- Decision cycles are short and seasonal (new year, school terms, job changes). Plan the calendar around the moments when people decide.

## B2B software sold to a team

Examples: platforms for HR, finance, operations.

- The reader is rarely the only decider. Content must give the champion words to use with their boss: the cost of the current way, the risk, what changes.
- Lead with the reader's operational problem, in their vocabulary. Product screens come second, as proof.
- Useful pillars: the problem and its cost, how-to for the job itself (useful even without the product), product in use (short demos, one task per piece), proof (cases and numbers, with authorization), point of view on the field.
- Specificity beats reach. A post that two hundred of the right people read is worth more than a viral one.
- The call to action matches the stage: follow or save for cold audiences, a trial or a demo for those comparing options.
- Never show real customer data in a screen capture; use a demonstration account.

## Adapting one idea across platforms

The idea carries over; the piece does not. For each platform change:

- **The opening.** A feed of strangers needs the hook in the first line or the first second. An audience of followers tolerates context first.
- **The length and the container.** One idea becomes a text post, a carousel of steps, or a 30-second video. Do not paste the LinkedIn text as an Instagram caption.
- **The register.** The voice stays; the formality shifts with the room.
- **The call to action.** Each platform has its own natural next step (comment, save, message, link in profile).
