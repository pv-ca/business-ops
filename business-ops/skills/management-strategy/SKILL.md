# Management and strategy

## Before you start

Read and follow `../business-context/references/coach-protocol.md` (a path relative to this skill's folder). It explains how to find the context, pick the company, what may be claimed and how to shape the answer. Context folder configured in the plugin: `${user_config.context_folder}`. When that shows no real path (it is empty, or the placeholder appears unreplaced because this agent has no plugin settings), the protocol says where to look. `references/books.md` lists the frameworks.

## How to work

1. **Diagnose before setting goals** (Rumelt). A strategy is a diagnosis of the main challenge, a guiding choice about how to face it, and coherent actions. A list of ambitions is not a strategy.
2. **Name the stage and the constraint.** A business still validating its offer needs learning goals, not growth targets. Read the stage from the company context; the readiness assessment in the `recurring-revenue` skill helps for recurring-revenue businesses.
3. **Choose few priorities:** three to five for the year, fewer for the quarter. Say explicitly what will not be done; that is where focus comes from.
4. **Give each priority** a measurable result, one owner and the first action. Use numbers the company can actually observe.
5. **Set the rhythm** (Harnish, Grove): a short weekly review of the priorities and a quarterly reset. Each meeting has a purpose and ends with decisions and owners.
6. **People:** clear roles before hiring; a hire solves a defined workload, not a vague feeling of overload. Feedback is specific and close to the event.

When the user runs more than one company, a portfolio question (where to put time and money) is legitimate: compare them openly, using each company's own context.

## Typical deliverables

Strategy on one page (diagnosis, choice, actions), quarterly plan, OKRs, meeting rhythm, role description, hiring scorecard, decision memo comparing options.

## Mistakes this skill prevents

- Goals with no diagnosis behind them.
- Ten priorities.
- Targets copied from companies at another stage.
- Hiring to fix a process problem.
