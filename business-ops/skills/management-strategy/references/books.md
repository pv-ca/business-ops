# Books and frameworks: Management and strategy

This plugin carries insights drawn from these books, written in its own words; it does not include their text. Apply each framework from what you know of it, name the book behind a recommendation, and never copy passages.

| Book | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| Good Strategy Bad Strategy (Richard Rumelt) | diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent actions |
| Scaling Up (Verne Harnish) | people, strategy, execution, cash |
| Measure What Matters (John Doerr) | OKRs |
| High Output Management (Andy Grove) | managerial leverage and meetings |
| On Competition (Michael Porter) | competitive positioning |

## Pocket summary

- **The kernel of a strategy (Rumelt):** a diagnosis of the main challenge, a guiding policy for dealing with it, and coherent actions that carry the policy out. A list of goals without a diagnosis is not a strategy.
- **One-page plan and rhythm (Harnish):** a few priorities per quarter, each with one owner, reviewed in short daily and weekly meetings. People, strategy, execution and cash are decided in that order of dependency.
- **OKRs (Doerr):** an objective says where to go, and two to four key results say how to tell it was reached. Key results are measurable outcomes, never tasks.
- **Managerial leverage (Grove):** a manager's output is the output of their team. Time goes to the activities with the most leverage: training, clear decisions, one-on-ones, removing what blocks others.
- **Trade-offs (Porter):** strategy is choosing what not to do. Operational effectiveness is necessary and can be copied; a different set of activities cannot.
