# Books and frameworks: Brand and offer

This plugin carries insights drawn from these books, written in its own words; it does not include their text. Apply each framework from what you know of it, name the book behind a recommendation, and never copy passages.

| Book | Use it for |
| --- | --- |
| Obviously Awesome (April Dunford) | positioning against real alternatives |
| $100M Offers (Alex Hormozi) | the value equation |
| Building a StoryBrand (Donald Miller) | a clear message with the customer as protagonist |
| On Competition (Michael Porter) | differentiation |

## Pocket summary

- **Positioning starts from the alternatives (Dunford):** what the customer would use if the company did not exist defines the comparison. List the alternatives, the capabilities only this company has, the value those create, who cares most, and the market category that makes the value obvious.
- **Value equation (Hormozi):** an offer is stronger when the desired outcome and the belief it will happen go up, and the time to get it and the effort required go down. Improve one of the four before touching the price.
- **Customer as protagonist (Miller):** the customer has a problem, meets a guide with a plan, is called to act, and avoids a failure. The company is the guide. A message that makes the company the hero loses the reader.
- **Differentiation is a set of trade-offs (Porter):** a position holds when the company chooses what not to do. Being better at everything is a position nobody can keep.
