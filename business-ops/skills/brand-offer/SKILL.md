# Brand and offer

## Before you start

Read and follow `../business-context/references/advisor-protocol.md` (a path relative to this skill's folder). It explains how to find the context, pick the company, what may be claimed and how to shape the answer. Context folder configured in the plugin: `${user_config.context_folder}`. When that shows no real path (it is empty, or the placeholder appears unreplaced because this agent has no plugin settings), the protocol says where to look. `references/books.md` lists the frameworks.

## How to work

1. **Start from the customer's real alternative.** What they would do if the company did not exist: a spreadsheet, a competitor, hiring someone, putting it off. Often the strongest competitor is the customer's current behaviour. Positioning is defined against that (Dunford).
2. **List what the company has that is different** and, for each item, the value it creates and for whom that value weighs most. Use only differentiators the context confirms.
3. **Write the positioning:** for whom, in which situation, what it solves, instead of what, with what proof. One sentence for internal use, then the customer-facing version.
4. **Build the offer** with the value equation (Hormozi): desired outcome, perceived likelihood of success, time to result, effort required. Improve the offer by moving those four before touching price. A free trial, a trial lesson or a guarantee serves to raise perceived likelihood and lower the anxiety of switching. The value equation diagnoses the offer; it is not permission to amplify claims in the copy. When it shows that a bigger outcome, more certainty, a shorter time or less effort would raise value, the answer is a change the company decides and can deliver (onboarding done for the customer, a real guarantee, a narrower promise kept), presented as an option for the user, never a sentence that promises it. Every outcome, deadline, probability, saving, ease, guarantee or risk reduction in the copy still passes the claim and promise test.
5. **Price and packages:** present by the unit the customer understands and tie each package to a usage situation. Prices, plans, discounts and conditions come only from the confirmed context; a proposed price change is delivered as a proposal, with the arithmetic and the risk.
6. **Message.** Let the communication problem choose the structure. StoryBrand's customer as protagonist and company as guide is a useful clarity check (is this about the customer, is the next step obvious), not a template every piece must fill: a message needs no character, villain, plan or transformation it does not use. For an experienced B2B buyer, opening with a diagnosis, an insight, a consequence, a reframe or a decision criterion often respects their time more than a story. Stock lines such as "we understand your challenge", "there is a better way" or "in just three steps" appear only when they say something true and specific. Copy for pages and campaign e-mails, the awareness stage and the signs of framework-shaped copy are in `references/messaging.md`.
7. **Voice:** three or four traits, each with an "is like this" and an "is not like this", plus sample sentences in the company's main channel.

## Claims and promises

This skill answers whether a claim or promise can be made, using the claim and promise test in section 3 of the protocol ("Evidence before framework" sits just above it). Run it on every customer-facing sentence before delivering, including the premises the message leans on, and say which test question a rejected claim failed and what was put in its place.

## Typical deliverables

Positioning statement and document, value proposition per audience, map of alternatives, offer and package table, one-page voice guide, homepage, landing page, offer page or campaign e-mail copy, review of a page or deck with every claim classified.
