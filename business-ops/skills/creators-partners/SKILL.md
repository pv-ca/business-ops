# Creators and partners

## Before you start

Read and follow `../business-context/references/coach-protocol.md` (a path relative to this skill's folder). It explains how to find the context, pick the company, what may be claimed and how to shape the answer. Context folder configured in the plugin: `${user_config.context_folder}`. When that shows no real path (it is empty, or the placeholder appears unreplaced because this agent has no plugin settings), the protocol says where to look. `references/books.md` lists the frameworks.

## How to work

1. **Define the objective** (reach, leads, sales, credibility) and what the partner gets out of it. A partnership that only benefits one side does not last.
2. **Clarify the partner's role.** A partner can be a buyer, a channel, a referrer, an influencer or only a dependency of the customer's process. Each calls for a different proposal, so establish which it is before writing one. When the company context marks a partner segment as a hypothesis, start with a small pilot.
3. **Evaluate fit** by audience overlap, real engagement and track record, not follower count.
4. **Write a clear briefing:** message, what may and may not be claimed (from the company context), format, deadline, disclosure rules.
5. **Keep the agreement simple and written:** deliverables, compensation, usage rights, duration. For contract terms and advertising-disclosure rules, verify current requirements and say when a lawyer is needed.
6. **Measure** with links, codes and a "how did you hear about us" question, against the objective set in step 1.

## Typical deliverables

Partner selection criteria and shortlist format, outreach message, briefing, partnership proposal, pilot plan, results report.

## Mistakes this skill prevents

- Choosing by audience size instead of audience fit.
- A briefing that lets the partner promise what the company cannot deliver.
- No way to attribute results.
- Scaling a partner program before one partnership worked.
